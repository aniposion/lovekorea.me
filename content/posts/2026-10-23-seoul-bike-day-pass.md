**Seoul Bike's ₩1,000 one-day pass gives you a 24-hour window for repeat rentals, but each rental must end within 60 minutes to avoid extra charges.** Keeping the same bike for a 90-minute outing can therefore cost more than buying the ₩2,000 two-hour option. Choose the pass around your longest ride between completed returns.

This is **Ttareungi (따릉이), Seoul's public bike-share service**. If you want a staffed riverside rental shop, use our separate [Han River bike-rental guide](/posts/han-river-activities-guide/).

## Two clocks: the pass and the ride

[Seoul's English fare guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/movement/seoul-public-bike/2-about-the-service/) starts the one-day validity period at the first rental. It also says that returning and renting again within the allowed ride time avoids overtime. Checked **October 23, 2026**.

**Worked timing example:** first unlock at 10:00 Monday means the pass window ends at 10:00 Tuesday. On the one-hour option, plan to finish the first rental before 11:00 Monday. A later rental has its own ride clock; returning does not restart the pass's 24-hour window.

For a relaxed first ride, set a reminder before the limit and choose a return station before leaving. A cafe stop is easier to enjoy after ending the rental than while watching a running timer.

## Which pass fits a 90-minute outing?

The [city's Korean tariff page](https://news.seoul.go.kr/traffic/archives/33719), revised **March 10, 2026** and checked October 23, lists these daily products for members and non-members:

| Daily product | Price | Time allowed per rental |
|---|---:|---:|
| One-hour | ₩1,000 | 60 minutes |
| Two-hour | ₩2,000 | 120 minutes |
| Three-hour | ₩3,000 | 180 minutes |

It lists overtime at **₩200 per five minutes** after the selected limit. The three-hour product is also documented in [Seoul's launch announcement](https://english.seoul.go.kr/ttareungi-hits-250-million-riders-in-ten-years-as-new-3-hour-pass-launches/).

**Hypothetical comparison for one rider, using the published tariff:**

| Your plan | Calculation | Total |
|---|---|---:|
| Keep one bike for 90 minutes on the one-hour pass | ₩1,000 + six 5-minute overtime units × ₩200 | **₩2,200** |
| Keep one bike for 90 minutes on the two-hour pass | ₩2,000, within its ride limit | **₩2,000** |
| Two 45-minute rides, with a completed return between them, on the one-hour pass | ₩1,000, both rides within the limit | **₩1,000** |

The last option suits a route with a convenient station break. It does not reserve the same bicycle for you. Do not plan an essential connection around immediate bike availability.

**Check the tourist purchase menu:** the general tariff includes a three-hour option, while [Visit Seoul's foreigner instructions](https://english.visitseoul.net/tours/Explore-Seoul-on-a-Seoul-Bike/18469), edited June 19, 2026, describe one-hour, two-hour and a separate full-day product. Buy the option actually offered in your flow and read its ride limit. The ₩1,000 calculations here do not describe every product called a day pass.

## Start in the foreigner flow

Visit Seoul directs visitors to the official **Ttareungi, Seoul Public Bicycle** app, then **Foreigner**, and says overseas-issued credit cards can be used. After purchase, its instructions use the station map and a bike's QR code to begin a rental.

[Seoul's illustrated tourist instructions](https://english.seoul.go.kr/seoul-bike-instructions-and-chatbot-service-for-international-tourists/) also show an email-based pass code. Save that email and the payment confirmation. Those screenshots date from November 2023, so button positions may differ now.

Before building an afternoon around a rental:

- Open the app on the connection you will use outdoors and make sure its station map works.
- Read the selected product's price, per-ride limit and extra-charge terms before paying.
- Keep your pass details accessible, with enough battery for the outing and return check.

The official overseas-card option is not a guarantee that every card or phone setup will work. If setup fails, a walk or a staffed rental shop is a practical fallback. Our [Korea eSIM and SIM guide](/posts/korea-esim-sim-incheon-airport-price-guide/) helps you check mobile-data preparation.

## Finish the return before walking away

The [official Seoul transport guide](https://english.visitseoul.net/seoul-transportation) says to return at a Ttareungi station and lock the bike. Visit Seoul's rental instructions specify lowering the lock lever fully, then checking the voice confirmation and phone message.

Treat the confirmation as part of your arrival. A bicycle standing beside other bicycles is not enough evidence that your rental ended.

If the return is not confirmed, stay with the bike and use the service's help instructions. Save the bike number, station, time and screen message so you can explain the problem clearly. Seoul's fare guide lists **1599-0120** for return problems; English telephone assistance is not verified here.

For your first outing, choose a short loop with a planned station stop, return before a long picnic, and keep an indoor alternative in our [rainy-day travel guide](/posts/korea-summer-rain-travel-guide/). The useful saving is choosing a pass you can comfortably use within its conditions.

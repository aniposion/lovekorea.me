**Free admission does not guarantee that the gallery or children's session you want is available.** Choose the museum for its subject and location, then check the specific room, program and date. That matters especially during the current gallery renovations in Seoul.

The three museums below serve different trips. Operating information checked October 3, 2026; use the linked notices again before travelling.

## Choose the subject first

| Museum | Best fit for this plan | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| National Museum of Korea, Yongsan, Seoul | A broad history-and-art visit built around selected galleries | Trying to finish the whole collection in a short afternoon |
| National Folk Museum of Korea, Jongno, Seoul | Everyday life and customs alongside a palace-area day | Assuming palace admission and museum entry are the same ticket |
| Gyeongju National Museum, Gyeongju | Context for a trip to the Silla historic sites | Adding an intercity journey solely because the museum is free |

These are suggested matches, not a visitor ranking or a claim that one museum is best for every family.

## National Museum of Korea: Monday is not a blanket closure day

The museum's [current visitor information](https://www.museum.go.kr/ENG/contents/E0101010000.do) lists **09:30–17:30 on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Sunday**, and **09:30–21:00 on Wednesday and Saturday**. Last admission is 30 minutes before closing. The permanent and special galleries have scheduled closures on the first Monday of March, June, September and December, with a stated exception for March 2026. January 1, Lunar New Year's Day and Chuseok are also closure dates.

Permanent galleries are free; particular special exhibitions may be charged. The Children's Museum uses reservations. Do not transfer the main museum's hours or entry rules to a children's session.

**Current gallery restriction:** the [renovation notice](https://www.museum.go.kr/ENG/contents/E0606010000.do?arcId=23711&catCustomType=united&catId=128&schM=view) identifies closures in parts of the calligraphy/painting and sculpture/crafts sections through January 28, 2027. Other galleries remain open. Look up the exact room if a particular object or collection is the reason for your visit; the museum being open is not enough.

## National Folk Museum: the late night is Wednesday

The Seoul museum's [visitor page](https://nfm.go.kr/english/subIndex/475.do) lists March–October hours of **09:00–18:00**, extending to **20:00 on Wednesday**, and November–February hours of **09:00–17:00**. Entry closes one hour before the listed closing time. It also announces **Permanent Exhibition Hall 1 closed September 30–December 21, 2026**. Exhibition admission is free; entering Gyeongbokgung Palace is a separate matter.

The [March 2026 notice](https://nfm.go.kr/user/bbs/english/16/439/bbsDataView/25676.do) explains the change from Saturday to Wednesday evening opening. The extension does not apply to the Children's Museum or Paju branch.

For children, consult the [separate visit instructions](https://www.nfm.go.kr/kids/eng/content.do?pageId=PAGE_000000000000092). Online reservations require Korean mobile verification; visitors who cannot reserve that way are directed to the information desk. This is an assistance route, not a guarantee of a place on arrival. Contact the museum beforehand if the session is essential to your day.

## Gyeongju National Museum: connect objects to the outdoor sites

If you are already visiting Gyeongju, reserve part of the day for its Silla collection. The [official visitor page](https://gyeongju.museum.go.kr/eng/html/sub01/0102.html) lists free admission to permanent galleries, with possible charges for ticketed special exhibitions. Check notices for the day's hours and room availability.

An effective editorial route is to see part of the historic district, visit the museum, then return to the landscape with a clearer idea of the objects and burial culture associated with it. Our [Gyeongju day-plan guide](/posts/gyeongju-history-travel-guide/) shows where the museum can replace an extra outdoor stop.

## A 90-minute visit that leaves something memorable

This is a planning exercise, not the museum's prescribed tour:

- **First 10 minutes:** pick one question and locate two relevant rooms on the current map.
- **Next 30 minutes:** follow the first room's main story. Read the introductory panel and choose two objects to examine closely.
- **Next 30 minutes:** use the second room to compare materials, uses or periods.
- **Last 20 minutes:** sit, revisit one label and allow time to leave without rushing.

Useful questions include “What changes between an everyday vessel and a ceremonial one?” or “Which objects connect to the site I saw this morning?” You do not need to photograph every label to have a worthwhile visit.

## Before setting out

Save the branch address, current closure notice and any reservation confirmation. Include the journey from the station entrance to the museum entrance in your travel time. For a paid exhibition, compare its subject and available session with your interests before paying; a free permanent gallery may already answer your question.

Pair one museum with a nearby activity, meal or [palace visit](/posts/korea-historic-palaces-guide/). If severe weather affects travel, use the [Seoul rain planning guide](/posts/korea-summer-rain-travel-guide/) before treating an indoor attraction as a reason to cross the city.

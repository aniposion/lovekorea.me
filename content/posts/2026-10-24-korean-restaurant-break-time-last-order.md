**A Korean restaurant can still be open after it has stopped accepting lunch orders.** Korea House's official full-course dining page lists lunch until **15:00**, but last orders at **13:00**. Under that schedule, arriving at **14:00 (2 p.m.)** is already an hour too late to start lunch. Check the **order cutoff and any afternoon break**, as well as closing time.

**Official pages checked October 24, 2026.** Korea House is a specific reservation-based dining example, not a universal Korean lunch schedule. The practical distinction matters when planning a late lunch anywhere: being inside the advertised opening hours does not confirm that a kitchen can still take your order.

## Last order, break time and closing are different

Look for these labels on the exact branch's current listing, booking page or entrance notice.

| Label | What to check before traveling |
|---|---|
| 영업시간 — opening hours | The overall business hours for your day; inspect any separate service periods |
| 라스트오더 / 주문 마감 — last order | The deadline for ordering; lunch and dinner may have different cutoffs |
| 브레이크타임 — break time | A pause between services; check both its start and reopening time |
| 입장 마감 — last admission | Whether seating or entry ends before orders do |
| 정기휴무 / 임시휴무 — regular / temporary closure | Whether the restaurant operates on your particular date |
| 재료 소진 — ingredients sold out | Whether the dish or service you want has ended early |

**A lunch last-order time can come before the break starts.** For example, a **hypothetical** restaurant might list lunch orders until 14:30 and a break from 15:00 to 17:00. Arriving at 14:50 misses the ordering deadline even though the break has not begun. These sample times are not a recommendation for a particular restaurant.

Do not assume you may sit inside throughout a break or that taking a queue number guarantees service before it. Ask the host whether the current queue will be served during this meal period.

## Two official examples show why closing time is not enough

### Korea House: last orders are two hours before lunch service ends

The operator's [Traditional Korean Cuisine visitor page](https://www.kh.or.kr/combine/content/list/913) lists **12:00–15:00 lunch, with last orders at 13:00**. Its [September 14 autumn notice](https://www.kh.or.kr/brd/board/1615/L/menu/1614?bbIdx=119821&brdType=R) confirms the lunch period and **Tuesday–Saturday** operation from September 15, 2026, with bookings through CATCHTABLE or telephone.

Use the booked arrival time and confirm availability; the 13:00 cutoff is not a promise of a walk-in table. For operating days, follow the newer dated notice rather than assuming the undated page's Monday closure is the only day off. The venue's other programs have their own arrangements.

If you book with a deposit, our [CATCHTABLE payment guide](/posts/catchtable-deposit-refund/) explains why the reservation charge may be refunded separately when you settle the full meal bill.

### Cafe Onion Anguk: a 22:00 close has a 21:30 last order

The café operator's [contact page](https://www.onionkr.com/category/contact/26/) lists the **Anguk branch** closing at **22:00**, with last orders at **21:30**. That leaves a **30-minute gap**: a 21:45 arrival is before closing but after the listed ordering cutoff.

This is a café example, not a guarantee that a full lunch or a particular bakery item is available. Check the branch and the item you actually want. Do not copy one location's hours to another.

## Calculate when to leave for your meal

Use the **earliest relevant deadline**: your reservation time, last admission or last order. Then subtract travel time and a realistic allowance for finding the entrance, waiting, seating and ordering.

**Hypothetical planning example:** a casual restaurant has a 14:30 lunch order cutoff. Your route takes 25 minutes; you allow 20 minutes for the entrance, possible queue and ordering.

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Lunch order cutoff | 14:30 |
| Subtract 20-minute planning allowance | Reach the restaurant by 14:10 |
| Subtract 25-minute journey | Leave by 13:45 |

The allowance is your planning choice, not a restaurant guarantee. A longer queue can still make the meal impossible. If the host cannot confirm service before the cutoff, choose another place or arrange a later meal instead of racing across the city.

For a reservation, follow its check-in and late-arrival terms. Do not substitute the restaurant's last-order time for your booked slot.

## Questions to show the staff

- **지금 식사 주문할 수 있나요?** — Can we order a meal now?
- **점심 주문은 몇 시까지 받으세요?** — Until what time do you take lunch orders?
- **브레이크타임이 있나요?** — Is there a break between services?
- **지금 대기하면 브레이크타임 전에 식사할 수 있나요?** — If we join the queue now, can we eat before the break?

Show your group size and expected arrival time with the question. A message saying “open until 9” still leaves the ordering deadline unanswered. For the meal itself, save our [Korean ordering and payment phrases](/posts/korean-restaurant-phrases-guide/).

## If you need food during an afternoon break

Choose a nearby alternative that confirms it is serving **now**. Check its food menu as well as its opening hours: an open café does not necessarily serve a substantial meal, and a food hall's building hours do not confirm every counter is still taking orders.

If time is short, an open convenience store with suitable ready-to-eat food can avoid another long journey. Our [convenience-store meal guide](/posts/korean-convenience-store-food-combos/) separates meals that need a microwave or hot water from options you can eat without heating.

Before leaving for a must-have meal, save **branch → date → reservation or order cutoff → break → nearby backup**. That is more useful than saving only the closing time.

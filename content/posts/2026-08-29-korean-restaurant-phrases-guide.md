Ordering food is one of the fastest ways to make beginner Korean useful. You do not need a perfect sentence: a polite opener, the dish name, a quantity, and **주세요** (*juseyo*, “please give me”) can carry most ordinary restaurant interactions.

This guide gives you 30 practical Korean restaurant phrases for choosing a dish, changing an order, explaining an allergy, paying, and requesting takeout. The Korean and natural English meanings were checked against resources from Korea’s National Institute of Korean Language, including its official [restaurant-ordering phrase sheet](https://www.korean.go.kr/attachFile/edu/forein03.pdf), on **August 29, 2026**. Romanization is a pronunciation bridge, not an exact sound system, so listen to native audio whenever possible.

Two polite building blocks appear repeatedly. **좀** (*jom*) softens a request, while **주세요** turns a noun or action into a practical “please.” In a noisy room, point first and speak second. The visual context prevents a correct phrase from being attached to the wrong dish.

## How to Order and Change a Dish

![Traveler using a quantity gesture while a server confirms the order](/images/korean-restaurant-phrases-guide-h2-1.webp)

At the table, get attention with **저기요** rather than calling a person by an English title. Some restaurants have a call button, and some expect customers to order at a kiosk or counter. Korea Tourism Organization’s current [Korean restaurant overview](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=903&vcontsId=229195) notes the mix of call bells, self-service water and banchan stations, table drawers, and unmanned ordering machines. Look for the system the restaurant is already using before waving.

When staff arrive, **주문할게요** means you are ready. Point to the exact menu item and say **이거 하나 주세요**. Change **하나** (*hana*, one) to **두 개** (*du gae*, two items) when appropriate. Portion rules differ: a barbecue menu may require a minimum number of servings, while a bowl of soup is usually ordered per person. Confirm the menu’s unit instead of assuming that “two” means two plates.

### A reusable order pattern

Use this sequence:

1. **저기요. 주문할게요.** — Excuse me. We’re ready to order.
2. Point to the exact line or photo.
3. **이거 두 개 주세요.** — Two of this, please.
4. Add one change: **소스는 따로 주세요.** — Sauce on the side, please.
5. Listen for a confirmation and check the quantity shown on the order screen or repeated by the server.

Do not stack five changes into one fast sentence. Ask for the main item and quantity first, then make one short request. If you are choosing among fermented sauces, our [gochujang, doenjang, and ssamjang guide](/posts/gochujang-doenjang-ssamjang-guide/) explains how their flavors and common uses differ.

### Asking about spice without making promises impossible

**이거 매워요?** asks whether a dish is spicy. The answer is subjective, so follow with a concrete request. **덜 맵게 해 주세요** asks for less heat; **안 맵게 해 주세요** asks for no heat. Neither guarantees that the kitchen can change a premixed broth, marinated meat, kimchi dish, or prepared sauce.

If the server says **안 돼요** (*an dwaeyo*, “it isn’t possible”), choose another item rather than repeating the request. For bibimbap, **고추장 빼 주세요** may be easy because the paste can be served separately. For a stew whose seasoning is already in the base, “no spice” may change the dish fundamentally or be unavailable.

### Asking for water, plates, and banchan

At many restaurants, water, utensils, or extra side dishes are self-service. Check the table drawer and nearby self-service station before calling staff. **반찬 더 주세요** is polite where refills are offered, but not every special side dish is unlimited or free. Korea Tourism Organization describes ordinary water and banchan refills as a common practice, yet posted restaurant rules and staff instructions always take priority.

## How to Explain Allergies and Dietary Needs

![Traveler showing an allergy message to an attentive restaurant server](/images/korean-restaurant-phrases-guide-h2-2.webp)

For a medical food allergy, speaking one memorized sentence is not enough. Say **알레르기가 있어요**, show a professionally translated Korean allergy card, name every ingredient you must avoid, and ask about broth, sauces, marinades, garnishes, and shared equipment. If staff cannot confirm, do not eat the uncertain dish.

The Ministry of Food and Drug Safety’s current [food-labeling overview](https://www.mfds.go.kr/eng/wpge/m_14/de011005l001.do) lists allergens that require declaration in covered packaged foods and explains mixture warnings for shared manufacturing processes. The agency also published a restaurant-focused [food-allergy prevention resource](https://www.mfds.go.kr/brd/m_512/view.do?Data_stts_gubun=C9999&company_cd=&company_nm=&itm_seq_1=0&multi_itm_seq=0&page=1&seq=31242&srchFr=&srchTo=&srchTp=0&srchWord=%EC%8B%9D%ED%92%88%EC%9A%A9+%EA%B8%B0%EA%B5%AC). These sources are helpful context, but a package label does not tell you everything about a restaurant recipe or cross-contact. Rules can also apply differently by business type, so never assume every independent restaurant displays a complete allergen matrix.

### Swap the ingredient in the pattern

Use **[ingredient] 알레르기가 있어요** for “I have a [ingredient] allergy.” Useful ingredient words include:

| English | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| Peanuts | 땅콩 | ttangkong |
| Tree nuts / nuts | 견과류 | gyeongwaryu |
| Milk | 우유 | uyu |
| Eggs | 계란 / 달걀 | gyeran / dalgyal |
| Wheat | 밀 | mil |
| Soybeans | 대두 / 콩 | daedu / kong |
| Sesame | 참깨 | chamkkae |
| Shrimp | 새우 | saeu |
| Crab | 게 | ge |
| Shellfish | 조개류 | jogaeryu |

Then ask **이거에 [ingredient]가 들어가요?** (“Does this contain [ingredient]?”). Korean particles change after some nouns, but staff will understand the ingredient plus **들어가요?** when you also show the written word. Clarity matters more than improvising perfect grammar.

### Allergy is different from preference

**안 먹어요** means “I don’t eat it”; it does not automatically communicate medical risk. For severe allergy, include **알레르기** and a clear statement such as **완전히 빼야 해요** (“I must completely avoid it”). Ask **같은 조리 기구를 사용해요?** about shared utensils or equipment. A shared grill, fryer, pot, knife, cutting board, or sauce container can matter even when the ingredient is not visible in the final dish.

Vegetarian, vegan, halal, and gluten-free needs also require detail. **고기 안 들어간 메뉴** asks for a dish without meat, but broth, fish sauce, anchovy stock, egg, dairy, or shared cooking surfaces may still be involved. State the ingredients you avoid rather than relying only on a broad label. If a restriction is medically serious and communication remains uncertain, choose a restaurant that documents ingredients or contact it before visiting.

## Paying, Receipts, Takeout, and Leftovers

![Traveler paying by card and receiving a receipt at a Korean restaurant](/images/korean-restaurant-phrases-guide-h2-3.webp)

In many Korean restaurants, you pay at the front counter after eating rather than waiting for a bill at the table. Bring the table number or order slip if one was provided. Say **계산할게요** as you approach the register. If you are unsure about payment, **카드 돼요?** is a short way to ask whether cards are accepted.

Routine tipping is not generally expected at Korean restaurant counters, according to Korea Tourism Organization. Pay the displayed total unless the business clearly operates another system. Do not add a tip by leaving loose cash on the table where it could be mistaken for forgotten money.

### Separate payment may not be available

**따로 계산할 수 있어요?** asks whether the group can pay separately. Ask before several people present cards. A busy restaurant or single-table order system may require one payment. If so, one person can pay and the group can settle privately afterward.

Ask **영수증 주세요** if you need a receipt. For takeout, **포장해 주세요** works when ordering. After dining, **남은 음식 포장해 주세요** asks whether leftovers can be packed, but the answer depends on the food and restaurant. Raw barbecue meat, buffet items, delicate dishes, and foods with storage risks may not be eligible. Once food leaves the restaurant, refrigerate it promptly and follow safe handling rather than carrying it for hours.

## Restaurant Manners That Make the Phrases Work

Polite Korean works best when your behavior supplies the rest of the message. Keep these habits simple:

- **Wait to be seated when the entrance has a queue system.** Some shops use a kiosk, a written list, or a host. Do not take an empty table until you understand the process.
- **Use the call bell once.** It may sound loud. Pressing repeatedly does not make service faster.
- **Check for self-service signs and stations.** Water, napkins, utensils, or banchan may be nearby. If you cannot read the sign, watch what other diners do or ask **셀프예요?** (*Selpeuyeyo?*, “Is it self-service?”).
- **Share banchan neatly.** Use serving utensils when provided and avoid hovering personal chopsticks over a shared dish.
- **Follow grill instructions.** Staff may cook the meat, replace the grill plate, or tell you when it is ready. Ask **지금 먹어도 돼요?** (*Jigeum meogeodo dwaeyo?*, “Can we eat it now?”) if uncertain.
- **Keep requests short during a rush.** Point, say one sentence, and confirm. A long translation-app paragraph can be harder to process than two precise questions.
- **Pay where the restaurant expects.** Watch whether other guests go to the counter or scan a table code. When unsure, ask **어디에서 계산해요?** (*Eodieseo gyesanhaeyo?*, “Where do I pay?”).

Korean meals often arrive as a main dish surrounded by several small plates. The tourism board’s [guide to the Korean meal table](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=140727) explains the role of rice, soup, kimchi, and banchan. Understanding that layout helps you avoid treating every small dish as a separately ordered appetizer.

## Three Mini-Dialogues You Can Reuse

These exchanges combine the phrase table into realistic sequences. Replace the dish or ingredient while keeping the structure.

### Ordering one dish with less spice

**Traveler:** 저기요. 주문할게요.

*Jeogiyo. Jumunhalgeyo.*

Excuse me. We’re ready to order.

**Traveler:** 이거 하나 주세요. 덜 맵게 해 주세요.

*Igeo hana juseyo. Deol maepge hae juseyo.*

One of this, please. Please make it less spicy.

**Server:** 네, 확인해 볼게요.

*Ne, hwaginhae bolgeyo.*

Okay, I’ll check.

The server has not promised the change; they have promised to check. Wait for the final confirmation.

### Checking a milk allergy

**Traveler:** 우유 알레르기가 있어요.

*Uyu alleoreugiga isseoyo.*

I have a milk allergy.

**Traveler:** 이거에 우유가 들어가요?

*Igeoe uyuga deureogayo?*

Does this contain milk?

**Traveler:** 같은 조리 기구를 사용해요?

*Gateun jori gigureul sayonghaeyo?*

Do you use the same cooking utensils?

Show the same questions in written Korean. If the answer is uncertain, select something else; politeness never requires accepting risk.

### Paying and requesting a receipt

**Traveler:** 계산할게요. 카드 돼요?

*Gyesanhalgeyo. Kadeu dwaeyo?*

I’d like to pay. Can I pay by card?

**Cashier:** 네.

*Ne.*

Yes.

**Traveler:** 영수증 주세요. 감사합니다.

*Yeongsujeung juseyo. Gamsahamnida.*

A receipt, please. Thank you.

## Pronunciation and Communication Tips

Do not chase perfect romanization in a noisy restaurant. Use it to remember the rhythm, then rely on the Hangul text for precision.

The polite ending **-요** is often the most audible part of a question. Keep it light rather than stretching it. In **주세요**, the first syllable sounds closer to *ju* than English “jew,” and the final **요** is a short *yo*. In **매워요** (*maewoyo*, spicy), the vowels flow together; native audio is more useful than spelling the word with extra English syllables.

Numbers can create the costliest misunderstanding. Pair the spoken quantity with your fingers, but remember that gestures are supporting evidence, not a replacement for checking the order screen or repeated quantity. If the total seems wrong, say **이거 두 개 맞아요?** (*Igeo du gae majayo?*, “Is this correctly two?”) while pointing to the item.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the easiest Korean phrase for ordering food?**

Point to the exact item and say **이거 하나 주세요** (*Igeo hana juseyo*), “One of this, please.” Change **하나** to **두 개** for two items, then confirm the quantity shown or repeated by staff.

**Q: How do I call a server politely in Korea?**

Say **저기요** (*jeogiyo*, “excuse me”) or press the table’s call bell once. First check whether the restaurant expects counter ordering, kiosk ordering, or table service.

**Q: How do I ask for non-spicy food in Korean?**

Ask **안 매운 메뉴 있어요?** (*An maeun menyu isseoyo?*, “Do you have a non-spicy dish?”). For a dish that can be adjusted, say **안 맵게 해 주세요**. A premixed sauce, broth, marinade, or kimchi-based dish may not be changeable, so choose another dish if staff say it is not possible.

**Q: Is saying an allergy phrase enough to stay safe?**

No. Say and show the Korean ingredient, explain that it is an allergy, ask about hidden ingredients and shared equipment, and do not eat the dish if staff cannot confirm. Carry your medication and emergency plan as directed by your clinician.

**Q: Do Korean restaurants expect tips?**

Routine tipping is not generally expected. Pay the displayed total at the counter or through the restaurant’s stated system. Follow any clearly explained policy at the specific business.

**Q: Can I ask a Korean restaurant to split the bill?**

You can ask **따로 계산할 수 있어요?**, but separate payments are not guaranteed. Ask before presenting multiple cards, and be ready for one person to pay the table total.

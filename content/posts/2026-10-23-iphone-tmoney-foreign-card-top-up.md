**A U.S.-issued card cannot fund T-money through Apple Wallet’s “Add Money” route: Apple requires a South Korea-issued credit or debit card.** Try the separate **MobileTmoney in-app Apple Pay route** with a listed network, or buy a physical T-money card and load Korean won cash. If you are already at the gate with an unfinished setup, use the cash fallback rather than keep retrying Wallet.

| Transit option | What card/money it needs | Where you set it up | What can go wrong | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-money in Apple Wallet, funded in Wallet | South Korea-issued credit or debit card | Wallet → add T-money / Add Money | A U.S.-issued card does not meet Apple’s rule | Travelers who also have an eligible Korean-issued card |
| T-money funded inside MobileTmoney with Apple Pay | An Apple Pay card on the app’s list: Mastercard, American Express, UnionPay, JCB | MobileTmoney’s international setup and in-app top-up | Visa is not listed; a listed network does not guarantee payment success | Visitors who want to tap their phone and can confirm a successful top-up |
| Physical T-money card loaded with cash | Buy the card, then add Korean won cash for fares | Convenience store; reload at a store or subway station | Buying the card alone does not fund rides; foreign-card reloads cannot be assumed | Visa-only travelers and anyone needing a practical backup |

## Check the current app and payment-card list

Use **MobileTmoney by Tmoney Co., Ltd. (모바일티머니)**. Its [App Store version history](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/mobiletmoney/id1470361790) shows **2.4.8, dated October 29**, with international use without sign-up and these Apple Pay networks: **Mastercard, American Express, UnionPay, JCB**.

**Visa is absent from that published list.** If it is your only card, plan the cash fallback. A card working for ordinary Apple Pay purchases does not establish T-money top-up eligibility: [Apple’s instructions, footnote 2](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/105079), apply the Korea-issued-card rule to Wallet, while the app publishes its own funding route.

## Set up before you reach the gate

Apple lists **iPhone XS/XR or later with iOS 17.2 or later**, or **Apple Watch Series 6/SE (2nd generation) or later with watchOS 10.2 or later**, as the minimum supported devices and software. [Apple’s T-money announcement](https://www.apple.com/kr/newsroom/2025/07/apple-and-tmoney-introduce-tmoney-for-apple-pay-on-iphone-and-apple-watch/) gives those requirements; its [support guide](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/105079) recommends the latest software.

Install and inspect the app before your trip, then finish setup and confirm a usable balance before reaching the gate. Follow its international, no-sign-up prompts and begin the top-up **inside MobileTmoney**. If you see only local identity verification, use the developer’s support rather than guessing through that process.

Check three separate numbers: the first two before approving payment, and the third immediately afterward:

1. **The amount of transit credit you requested.** This is the balance you intend to spend on travel.
2. **The total payment shown.** Read any fee displayed in the payment preview and check your own card’s overseas-payment terms.
3. **The balance after completion.** Confirm that the intended transit card received the money before entering the queue.

Start with a modest amount suited to your immediate journey and keep the confirmation for resolving a mismatch.

| What happened | Next action |
|---|---|

| The app shows no usable visitor setup | Record the app version, phone model and exact message for support |
| The app rejects a listed network | Check the issuer’s transaction status and the app error; the network logo alone cannot explain the decline |
| A charge appears but the transit balance has not changed | Retain the confirmation and ask support to reconcile the transaction before loading more |
| Departure is approaching and setup is unfinished | Use the physical-card fallback below and troubleshoot away from the gate |

The developer lists **mobileteam@tmoney.co.kr** for help on its official app page. These are troubleshooting suggestions, not guarantees that a particular setting will fix a decline.

## Choose the device you will tap

After setup, check which transit card is selected for **Express Mode**. Apple explains that an eligible Express Mode card can be used without waking or unlocking the device. [Apple’s Express Mode guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/105079) covers that behavior.

**One T-money card can be used on only one device at a time.** To use an iPhone and Apple Watch separately, Apple requires different cards on the two devices; Wallet does not transfer balance between those separate cards. Decide which device will carry your journey’s balance before you leave. [Apple’s Korean transit FAQ](https://www.apple.com/kr/apple-pay/transit/) explains the distinction.

Traveling together? Read the [one card for two people guide](/posts/one-tmoney-card-two-people/) before deciding how many fare cards to prepare.

## Keep a workable fallback

[Tmoney’s visitor guide](https://blog.tmoney.co.kr/en/tmoney-guide/tmoney-convenience-store-guide/) confirms that physical cards are sold at convenience stores and can be loaded at stores and subway stations without an app or registration. Bring Korean won and ask the cashier to confirm the loaded balance. Tmoney recommends cash as the reliable reload option; methods vary by location, so do not count on a U.S. card at the counter.

Landing at Incheon and taking a bus? Check [paying the airport limousine bus with T-money versus buying a ticket](/posts/incheon-airport-limousine-bus-ticket-vs-tmoney-guide/) before choosing how much transit credit to load.

For heavy Seoul sightseeing, compare a [short-term Climate Card with T-money](/posts/seoul-climate-card-vs-tmoney/) by your actual trips. If considering WOWPASS as a backup, read about its [shopping and T-money balances](/posts/wowpass-tmoney-separate-balances/) before choosing it.

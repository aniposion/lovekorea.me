**An empty table and a power outlet do not tell you a cafe's seating rules.** Before settling in, look for purchase, time-limit or laptop notices. A short question to staff is more useful than a supposed rule for every cafe in Korea.

This guide provides translations and a practical visit sequence. It does not claim that every business has the same policies.

## The signs most likely to change your plans

| Sign | Meaning | What to clarify |
|---|---|---|
| 1인 1음료 | One drink per person | Whether a dessert counts, and whether there are exceptions for children |
| 1인 1메뉴 | One menu item per person | Which items qualify |
| 이용 시간 2시간 | Two-hour use limit | When the period starts and which seats it applies to |
| 노트북 사용 금지 | No laptop use | Whether it covers the whole cafe or only a marked area |
| 외부 음식 반입 금지 | No outside food | Ask first about a necessary exception |
| 반납대 | Return counter | Where cups and trays should go |
| 예약석 | Reserved seating | Choose another table or ask staff |

The two-hour sign is an example translation, not a national time limit. Follow the wording at the business you enter.

## A visit sequence that works without guessing

**At the entrance:** check whether staff seat guests, there is a waiting list, or the cafe uses counter ordering. If you want to sit first, ask **“먼저 앉아도 될까요?”** (“May I sit first?”). Keep valuables with you rather than leaving a passport or laptop as a reservation marker.

**At ordering:** decide the item, hot or iced, and eat-in or takeout. Point to a menu item and say **“이거 하나 주세요.”** (“One of this, please.”). The staff may confirm size or other options; customization is subject to the shop's menu.

**After payment:** keep the receipt or order number. If given a pager, follow the pickup instructions and return it when collecting your order. Leave room at the register for the next customer.

**Before leaving:** locate the return station. If service is at the table or you cannot see one, ask **“쟁반은 어디에 두면 될까요?”** (“Where should I put the tray?”). Follow the marked liquid and waste bins instead of pouring drinks into a general bin.

## An ordering card to save

| Korean | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 아이스로 주세요. | Iced, please. |
| 따뜻한 걸로 주세요. | Hot, please. |
| 매장에서 마실게요. | I'll drink it here. |
| 포장해 주세요. | To go, please. |
| 덜 달게 가능할까요? | Could it be made less sweet? |
| 카드로 결제할 수 있나요? | Can I pay by card? |
| 영수증 주세요. | Please give me a receipt. |

If a foreign card fails, ask about another payment method rather than assuming a particular wallet or card works everywhere. For allergies, use a prepared Korean allergy card and confirm ingredients and cross-contact. A single “Does it contain milk?” question may not resolve all preparation risks; see [restaurant phrases](/posts/korean-restaurant-phrases-guide/).

## Decide whether this is a coffee stop or a work session

For a brief stop, a small specialty bar may suit your plans even without Wi-Fi. For work, confirm laptop permission, time limits and power access before ordering. Ask **“여기서 노트북을 사용해도 될까요?”** (“May I use a laptop here?”).

Keep cables out of aisles, use headphones and take calls where permitted. Do not unplug another customer's charger or cafe equipment. An extra order does not override a stated time limit. If you need guaranteed workspace for several hours, choose a venue that explicitly offers it.

For a group, check seating before moving furniture or joining tables. For photographs, ask **“사진 찍어도 될까요?”** (“May I take photos?”) and avoid making other customers the subject without permission. A cafe may allow a quick phone photo while declining tripods or a commercial shoot.

## Tipping and general courtesy

[Visit Seoul's official etiquette guide](https://english.visitseoul.net/essential-Info-article/Etiquette/ENN000393) describes tipping as not customary. That broad guidance does not establish a particular cafe's service charges or payment screen. Read the displayed total and ask if a charge is unclear. Source checked October 3, 2026.

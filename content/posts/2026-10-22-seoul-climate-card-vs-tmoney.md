**A ₩20,000 seven-day Climate Card costs ₩23,000 if you need the physical card.** In our base-fare example, 14 subway journeys cost ₩21,700 on a T-money card you already own. The unlimited pass would cost ₩1,300 more, before any payment fees. Count the journeys your pass will actually replace before buying it.

**Official sources checked October 22, 2026.** The comparisons below are our calculations for an adult visitor, not a promise about your route or a record of a personal trip.

## Start with the card you already own

[Korea Tourism Organization's transport-card guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=140663) lists a standard physical Climate Card at ₩3,000, separate from the pass. Keep that purchase cost separate from the money loaded for travel.

| Your starting point | What to compare |
|---|---|
| You have T-money but no Climate Card | Expected covered fares versus the pass, a new Climate Card and payment fees |
| You already have both cards | Expected covered fares versus the pass and payment fees; the old card purchases are already paid for |
| You have neither card | Include the actual purchase cost of each card you need; do not assume T-money itself is free |
| You will need T-money for other journeys anyway | Its purchase cost belongs in both plans; compare the extra Climate Card expense with the fares it replaces |

KTO lists ordinary T-money cards at ₩3,000–₩5,000, depending on the card. Use the price of the specific card you intend to buy, especially for a souvenir design.

## How many subway journeys make the pass cheaper?

The short-term loads are ₩5,000, ₩8,000, ₩10,000, ₩15,000 and ₩20,000 for 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 days respectively. These prices and the separate card cost are listed in the [KTO guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=140663).

**Calculation assumptions:** you already own T-money; every journey is within Climate Card coverage and costs the ordinary adult subway card base fare of **₩1,550**, as listed in [Seoul's current subway fare table](https://english.seoul.go.kr/policy/transportation/modes-of-transport/subway/). There are no payment fees, distance supplements or discounts in this simplified example. The counts are totals over the pass period, not daily targets.

**Journey thresholds:** “Card owned” means an existing Climate Card; “New card” includes its ₩3,000 purchase. Each count is the first total at which the pass costs less.

| Pass | Load | Card owned | New card |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 day | ₩5,000 | 4 | 6 |
| 2 days | ₩8,000 | 6 | 8 |
| 3 days | ₩10,000 | 7 | 9 |
| 5 days | ₩15,000 | 10 | 12 |
| 7 days | ₩20,000 | 13 | 15 |

For example, a new card plus a seven-day load costs **₩23,000**. Fourteen base-fare journeys cost **14 × ₩1,550 = ₩21,700**; fifteen cost **₩23,250**. The pass first becomes cheaper at 15 journeys, with just **₩250** of headroom before fees.

If you already own the Climate Card, the same seven-day load costs ₩20,000. Thirteen journeys cost ₩20,150, so the decision changes without changing the itinerary.

### Count paid journeys, not every train you board

A subway transfer can be part of the same paid journey. Do not count a change of lines as another ₩1,550 saving. Bus–subway transfers also have integrated-fare rules; Seoul's fare guide specifies tapping on and off and a transfer time limit, normally 30 minutes, or 60 minutes from 9 p.m. to 7 a.m.

For your own comparison, add the actual pay-as-you-go fares of **covered journeys during the pass's validity**, then compare that total with the pass, any additional card purchase and fees. Longer trips, bus fares and discounted fares need their own amounts. Money loaded onto T-money and left unspent is not a fare already consumed.

## Foreign-card payment can change a close result

Seoul's [March 17, 2026 payment announcement](https://english.seoul.go.kr/climate-cards-and-single-journey-transit-tickets-now-accepting-international-credit-cards-no-cash-needed/) says the new vending machines at 273 stations on Lines 1–8 accept internationally issued credit and debit cards for Climate Card purchases and short-term loads. It also states an **average service fee of approximately 3.7%**. Check the machine's actual total before confirming payment; your card issuer may have separate charges.

**Illustrative fee calculation:** if 3.7% were applied to a ₩23,000 card-and-pass total, the cost would become **₩23,851**. Against fifteen ₩1,550 journeys costing ₩23,250, the pass would then cost **₩601 more**. This is a sensitivity example using the published average, not an exact machine quote.

That small margin is a good reason to compare the final amount rather than planning extra trips just to justify a pass. The announcement covers Climate Card and single-journey ticket payments; it does not establish that an ordinary T-money balance can be topped up with your foreign bank card.

## Check your airport direction and uncovered trips

Use [Seoul's Climate Card coverage guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/policy/transportation/climate-card/) for the exact stations and bus operator before treating a fare as a saving.

| Journey | How to budget it |
|---|---|
| Covered Seoul station → Incheon Airport T1/T2 on AREX All Stop | Getting off at the airport is permitted with a valid pass after a qualifying journey |
| Incheon Airport T1/T2 → Seoul | Boarding at those airport stations is not covered; budget the arrival fare separately |
| AREX Express or airport limousine bus | Budget separately; the Climate Card is not the required ticket |
| Sinbundang Line or another uncovered journey | Exclude it from the pass-saving calculation |

The [AREX comparison](/posts/incheon-airport-arex-guide/) explains Express versus All Stop. For an airport bus, check the [ticket and T-money payment guide](/posts/incheon-airport-limousine-bus-ticket-vs-tmoney-guide/) for the particular service. A Climate Card does not replace every transport payment on your trip.

## A late recharge can waste most of a one-day pass

Short-term validity starts on the charging date. Seoul's example says a one-day pass runs through that date's last bus/subway service; it is **not a rolling 24-hour pass**.

**Planning example:** if you reach central Seoul late in the evening and need only one more ride, compare that fare with a pass that starts that evening. Loading it the following morning may fit your sightseeing day better. Buying the physical card and loading a pass are separate decisions.

KTO also says a short-term pass cannot be changed or reloaded until it expires. For Seoul → Busan → Seoul, compare separate Seoul travel blocks instead of assuming one uninterrupted pass will suit the whole trip.

## The tourist passes still exist after the 2026 change

The resident-focused monthly transition caused confusion, but Seoul's [August 20 transition notice](https://english.seoul.go.kr/smg-steps-up-outreach-ahead-of-climate-pass-launch-on-september-1-encouraging-early-card-conversion/) explicitly retains **1-, 2-, 3-, 5- and 7-day Climate Card passes**. The new Climate Pass benefits are based on Seoul residence. A visitor comparing short-term transit should use the tourist-pass prices above rather than assuming a resident monthly reimbursement applies.

## Choose for the trip you will actually take

- **Mostly walking around one area each day:** price the outward and return journeys first. Unlimited travel may add little value.
- **Several covered journeys on consecutive Seoul days:** use the threshold table, then replace its flat fare with your route estimates.
- **Mixed cities, uncovered lines or uncertain plans:** keep a separate transport budget; count only the fares the Climate Card can remove.
- **Considering a sightseeing bus:** compare its experience and route using our [city tour bus versus subway guide](/posts/seoul-city-tour-bus-vs-subway/). An attraction-style tour ticket solves a different travel decision.

Choose the pass when it suits both the route and the final cost. A small expected saving is not a reason to reorganize a holiday around extra train rides.

**SPF 50+ does not tell you whether a sunscreen will stay effective while swimming or sweating.** Before a beach day, find the water-resistance instructions on the actual product. A light texture, blue packaging or the word “sun” is not a substitute for that label.

This is a label-reading and shopping guide, not a wear test or ranking of Korean formulas. Sun-protection guidance was checked against the American Academy of Dermatology on October 4, 2026. Follow the directions for the market version you actually buy.

## The three checks before brand or finish

The [AAD’s selection guidance](https://www.aad.org/spot-skin-cancer/learn-about-skin-cancer/prevent-skin-cancer/how-to-select-a-sunscreen) recommends **SPF 30 or higher, broad-spectrum protection and water resistance**. Sunscreen also belongs with shade and protective clothing; it is not a reason to remain in intense sun indefinitely.

| Label detail | What to establish | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| SPF | The product’s stated sunburn-protection rating | How long you personally can stay outside without reapplication |
| UVA / broad-spectrum information | Protection information under that market’s labeling system | That any similarly named export product has the same formula |
| Water resistance | Instructions and any stated duration | Waterproof or all-day protection |
| Directions | Amount, application, reapplication and warnings | That makeup applied over it replaces those steps |

PA markings commonly appear on Korean products as UVA information. Do not convert the number of plus signs into hours of protection. Check the full package and official manufacturer information when comparing different markets.

## Buy for the activity you have planned

| Your day | Shopping priority | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Mostly indoors with short outdoor journeys | A suitable formula you can apply as directed | Using less because you prefer how foundation looks |
| Beach, hiking or a sweaty outdoor day | Explicit water-resistance and reapplication instructions | Assuming a daily cosmetic sunscreen has those properties |
| Sensitive or reactive skin | Your known tolerances and a small first purchase | Treating “natural” or “sensitive” as an allergy-free guarantee |
| Long outdoor day with limited bag space | Enough product and a practical way to reapply | Counting a tiny sample as a full-day supply without checking usage |

Under US labeling, the [AAD explains water resistance as 40 or 80 minutes](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/sun-protection/shade-clothing-sunscreen/understand-sunscreen-labels). Reapply outdoors at least every two hours and after towel drying; follow the label after swimming or sweating. No sunscreen is waterproof. Read the actual Korean or export package instead of transferring a US claim to an unnamed product.

## Cream, stick, cushion or spray?

Choose a format you can use according to its directions. A cream or lotion lets you spread an even layer; a stick may be convenient for carrying, but a single swipe is not automatically the labeled application. With a spray, follow its inhalation, wind and application warnings rather than spraying toward your face or other people.

SPF makeup is still makeup applied in whatever amount gives your preferred finish. Do not assume that amount delivers the protection stated on a sunscreen used as directed. Our [cushion comparison](/posts/cushion-vs-foundation-korean-makeup/) covers shade, refills and format separately.

For an unfamiliar cosmetic, the [AAD’s small-area testing guide](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/prevent-skin-problems/test-skin-care-products/) explains a seven-to-ten-day check. It does not prove long-term tolerance or replace medical evaluation of a reaction. If a product causes irritation, stop using it and obtain appropriate advice.

## A Korean product name can cover different market versions

Before comparing a Korean shelf product with an overseas listing, record the full name, size, sales market, ingredients and protection label. Similar branding is not proof that two versions are identical. Buy through a channel you can identify and keep the package and receipt.

[Olive Young Korea](https://www.oliveyoung.co.kr/) and [Olive Young Global](https://global.oliveyoung.com/) are different shopping channels. A US-dollar Global offer does not establish the won price, stock or promotion in a Seoul branch. Current promotions and destination restrictions must be checked for the channel you will actually use.

Compare the final checkout total for the same product and quantity. Before counting a discount, check its expiry, eligible items, minimum spend and whether it applies in store or online.

## Count usable sunscreen, not every item in the gift set

**Hypothetical comparison, not live retail prices:**

| Offer | Price | Sunscreen quantity | Price per ml |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One 50 ml tube | ₩15,000 | 50 ml | ₩300 |
| Two identical 50 ml tubes plus a 20 ml cleanser | ₩26,000 | 100 ml | ₩260 |

The second offer is cheaper per milliliter of sunscreen, but costs ₩11,000 more upfront. The cleanser is not another 20 ml of sunscreen. Count both tubes only if their formula, expiry and amount suit your use. Include shipping or additional travel when comparing final costs.

For a new formula, avoiding a large unsuitable bundle can matter more than a small unit-price saving. For a formula you already tolerate and use, the larger pack may make sense. ## Save these five details before leaving the store

1. Exact product and market version.
2. Protection and water-resistance labeling for your activity.
3. Application and reapplication directions you can read.
4. Usable quantity, expiry and final price.
5. Seller, receipt and opened-product return conditions.

Choose the product only after those checks. No filter type, texture or popularity label establishes that it is the best sunscreen for everyone.

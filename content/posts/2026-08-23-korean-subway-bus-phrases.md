## Korean Transit Phrases at a Glance

![Traveler asking a subway information attendant for directions](/images/korean-subway-bus-phrases-h2-0.webp)

You do not need a full Korean sentence to survive a subway transfer or ask a bus driver about a stop. A polite opener, one clear place name, and a useful verb are usually enough. The phrases below are designed for moments when a map is confusing, a card does not work, or you want to confirm that you are heading the right way.

Romanization is only a pronunciation bridge. Korean sounds do not map perfectly onto English, so listen to native audio when possible. Still, a traveler who speaks slowly and shows the Korean sentence on a phone will usually communicate more clearly than someone trying to improvise a long English explanation.

| Korean | Romanization | Natural English | Best moment to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 저기요. | Jeogiyo. | Excuse me. | Get someone’s attention politely |
| 도와주세요. | Dowajuseyo. | Please help me. | You are stuck or confused |
| 이 역이 맞아요? | I yeogi majayo? | Is this the right station? | Confirm a station |
| 이 버스가 맞아요? | I beoseuga majayo? | Is this the right bus? | Confirm a bus before boarding |
| 서울역에 가요? | Seoul-yeoge gayo? | Does this go to Seoul Station? | Confirm the destination |
| 어디에서 타요? | Eodieseo tayo? | Where do I board? | Find a platform or stop |
| 어디에서 내려요? | Eodieseo naeryeoyo? | Where do I get off? | Confirm your stop |
| 어디에서 갈아타요? | Eodieseo garatayo? | Where do I transfer? | Find a transfer point |
| 몇 번 출구예요? | Myeot beon chulgu-yeyo? | Which exit number is it? | Leave a subway station |
| 얼마나 걸려요? | Eolmana geollyeoyo? | How long does it take? | Ask about travel time |

Three verbs unlock most of the system: **타다 (*tada*)**, to ride or board; **내리다 (*naerida*)**, to get off; and **갈아타다 (*garatada*)**, to transfer. These are the same core actions used in beginner materials from Korea’s National Institute of Korean Language and King Sejong Institute. If you remember only one sequence, remember **타요 → 내려요 → 갈아타요**: board, get off, transfer.

The ending **-요** makes everyday speech polite without sounding stiff. Use it with drivers, station staff, and strangers. You may hear shorter or more casual forms between friends, but public transit is a place where polite Korean is the safest default.

## Subway Phrases for Tickets, Platforms, and Transfers

![Traveler changing subway lines in a Seoul transfer corridor](/images/korean-subway-bus-phrases-h2-1.webp)

Korean subway stations often provide English on maps, machines, and announcements, especially in major cities. The hard part is usually not buying a ride; it is confirming the correct platform, transfer direction, or exit in a large station. Use a destination name as the anchor of your question.

### Finding the correct platform

**강남역에 가려면 어디로 가요?**

*Gangnam-yeoge garyeomyeon eodiro gayo?*

Which way do I go for Gangnam Station?

**이쪽이 홍대입구역 가는 방향이에요?**

*Ijjo-gi Hongdae-ipgu-yeok ganeun banghyang-ieyo?*

Is this the direction for Hongik University Station?

**몇 호선을 타요?**

*Myeot hoseoneul tayo?*

Which line do I take?

The word **방향 (*banghyang*)** means direction. **몇 호선 (*myeot hoseon*)** asks which numbered subway line. When station names feel difficult to pronounce, show the Korean name on your map and say **여기 가요? (*Yeogi gayo?*)**, “Does this go here?” It is short, but the screen supplies the missing context.

### Making a transfer

**어디에서 2호선으로 갈아타요?**

*Eodieseo i-hoseoneuro garatayo?*

Where do I transfer to Line 2?

**한 번 갈아타요?**

*Han beon garatayo?*

Do I transfer once?

**환승하는 곳이 어디예요?**

*Hwanseunghaneun gosi eodiyeyo?*

Where is the transfer area?

**환승 (*hwanseung*)** is the noun for a transfer; **갈아타다** is the everyday verb “to change rides.” Either works. For a beginner, the pattern **[line]으로 갈아타요?** is especially reusable: replace the bracket with *i-hoseon* for Line 2, *sam-hoseon* for Line 3, and so on.

### Exits and station facilities

**명동은 몇 번 출구예요?**

*Myeongdongeun myeot beon chulgu-yeyo?*

Which exit is for Myeongdong?

**엘리베이터가 어디예요?**

*Ellibeiteoga eodiyeyo?*

Where is the elevator?

**화장실이 어디예요?**

*Hwajangsiri eodiyeyo?*

Where is the restroom?

An exit number can save a long walk because major intersections may have many exits. If you have luggage, a stroller, or limited mobility, ask for the elevator before following the nearest staircase. ## Bus Phrases for Boarding and Getting Off

![Traveler pressing a stop-request button inside a Seoul city bus](/images/korean-subway-bus-phrases-h2-2.webp)

Buses create more speaking pressure than subways because you must choose the correct curb, board in the right direction, and request your stop while the vehicle is moving. Ask before tapping your card if you are unsure. A bus number plus a destination is enough to build a useful question.

### Confirming the bus and stop

**이 버스가 경복궁에 가요?**

*I beoseuga Gyeongbokgunge gayo?*

Does this bus go to Gyeongbokgung?

**이 정류장이 맞아요?**

*I jeongnyujangi majayo?*

Is this the right bus stop?

**몇 정거장 남았어요?**

*Myeot jeonggeojang namasseoyo?*

How many stops are left?

**다음 정류장에서 내려요?**

*Daeum jeongnyujangeseo naeryeoyo?*

Do I get off at the next stop?

**정류장 (*jeongnyujang*)** means bus stop, while **정거장 (*jeonggeojang*)** is a more general stop or station counter used naturally when counting stops. If pronunciation fails, say the destination, then **가요?** with rising question intonation.

### Asking the driver for help

**도착하면 알려 주세요.**

*Dochakamyeon allyeo juseyo.*

Please let me know when we arrive.

**여기에서 내려도 돼요?**

*Yeogieseo naeryeodo dwaeyo?*

Can I get off here?

**문 좀 열어 주세요.**

*Mun jom yeoreo juseyo.*

Please open the door.

The last phrase is for an unusual situation when the bus is stopped and the door has not opened; it is not a substitute for pressing the stop button in advance. Seoul’s official visitor guidance tells riders to press the bell before their destination and to tap their transportation card when both boarding and leaving a bus if they want the integrated transfer discount.

As checked on **August 23, 2026**, Seoul’s official public-transport information says eligible card transfers generally must begin within 30 minutes after tapping out, extended to 60 minutes during the overnight window stated by the city, and allow up to four transfers. These details are time-sensitive and Seoul-specific, so verify the [current Seoul Metropolitan Government public transportation guidance](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/movement/public-transportation/) rather than applying them automatically in every Korean city.

## Transit Card and Problem-Solving Phrases

![Traveler receiving help with a transit card at a subway service counter](/images/korean-subway-bus-phrases-h2-3.webp)

When a gate rejects your card, do not keep tapping rapidly. Step aside so other passengers can pass, then show the card to staff. The most useful pattern is **-이/가 안 돼요 (*an dwaeyo*)**, meaning something does not work.

**교통카드 충전해 주세요.**

*Gyotongkadeu chungjeonhae juseyo.*

Please top up this transportation card.

**이 카드가 안 돼요.**

*I kadeuga an dwaeyo.*

This card is not working.

**잔액이 얼마예요?**

*Janaegi eolmayeyo?*

How much balance is left?

**여기에서 교통카드를 살 수 있어요?**

*Yeogieseo gyotongkadeureul sal su isseoyo?*

Can I buy a transportation card here?

**잘못 찍었어요.**

*Jalmot jjigeosseoyo.*

I tapped incorrectly.

**추가 요금이 있어요?**

*Chuga yogeumi isseoyo?*

Is there an additional fare?

**영수증 주세요.**

*Yeongsujeung juseyo.*

Please give me a receipt.

The general word **교통카드 (*gyotongkadeu*)** avoids assuming which card product a retailer or city supports. Official Korea Tourism Organization guidance lists rechargeable options and notes that availability, purchase locations, service areas, and refund rules differ. Check the [current VISITKOREA transportation-card guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=140663) before buying a time-limited pass or planning a refund.

If you pass the wrong gate or miss a stop, clarity matters more than perfect grammar:

**반대 방향으로 탔어요.**

*Bandae banghyangeuro tasseoyo.*

I went in the opposite direction.

**정류장을 지나쳤어요.**

*Jeongnyujangeul jinachyeosseoyo.*

I missed my bus stop.

**어떻게 가야 돼요?**

*Eotteoke gaya dwaeyo?*

How should I get there?

Show your intended destination after the last question. Staff can then point you to a gate, platform, or return route without guessing what went wrong.

## How to Pronounce the Phrases So People Understand

Perfect pronunciation is not required, but several habits make beginner Korean much easier to understand.

1. **Say the destination first or show it on screen.** Place names carry the most important information.
2. **Keep each phrase short.** “Seoul-yeok, eodieseo tayo?” is easier to process than a memorized paragraph.
3. **Do not pronounce every romanized letter like English.** In *jeongnyujang*, sounds blend; native audio is more reliable than spelling.
4. **Use a gentle rising tone for yes-or-no questions.** *Majayo?* should sound like confirmation, not a statement.
5. **Keep the polite ending.** The final *-yo* in *gayo, tayo,* and *naeryeoyo* does useful social work.
6. **Pause between the place and the question.** “Busan-yeok … eodieseo naeryeoyo?” gives the listener time to identify the destination.

The spelling **갈아타요** is often romanized *garatayo*, but the middle sounds connect more smoothly than an English reader may expect. **알려 주세요** is written *allyeo juseyo* and means “please let me know.” For structured listening and grammar practice, King Sejong Institute’s official beginner materials include transport exercises using **걸려요** (it takes), **타다** (ride), and **갈아타다** (transfer).

Do not worry about sounding like a native speaker. Your practical goal is to be polite, audible, and unambiguous. A clear phone screen plus five Korean words beats flawless pronunciation of the wrong station.

## Three Mini-Dialogues You Can Reuse

### 1. Confirming a subway direction

**Traveler:** 저기요. 강남역에 가려면 이쪽이 맞아요?

*Jeogiyo. Gangnam-yeoge garyeomyeon ijjogi majayo?*

Excuse me. Is this the right way to Gangnam Station?

**Local:** 네, 2호선 타세요.

*Ne, i-hoseon taseyo.*

Yes, take Line 2.

**Traveler:** 어디에서 갈아타요?

*Eodieseo garatayo?*

Where do I transfer?

### 2. Checking a bus stop

**Traveler:** 이 버스가 북촌에 가요?

*I beoseuga Bukchone gayo?*

Does this bus go to Bukchon?

**Driver:** 네, 타세요.

*Ne, taseyo.*

Yes, get on.

**Traveler:** 도착하면 알려 주세요. 감사합니다.

*Dochakamyeon allyeo juseyo. Gamsahamnida.*

Please let me know when we arrive. Thank you.

### 3. Fixing a card problem

**Traveler:** 저기요. 이 카드가 안 돼요.

*Jeogiyo. I kadeuga an dwaeyo.*

Excuse me. This card is not working.

**Staff:** 잔액이 부족해요.

*Janaegi bujokhaeyo.*

The balance is too low.

**Traveler:** 만 원 충전해 주세요.

*Man won chungjeonhae juseyo.*

Please add 10,000 won.

In the last dialogue, replace **만 원** with the amount you want. If numbers make you nervous, show the amount on your phone and simply say **충전해 주세요**.

## Transit Etiquette That Supports Better Communication

Language works best when your behavior makes the request easy to answer. Move away from gates and bus doors before opening a map. Let passengers exit first. Keep your phone brightness high enough for staff to read the destination, but do not push it close to someone’s face.

On a bus, press the stop-request button with enough time for the driver to stop safely. Stand only when it is safe, hold a rail, and keep bags out of the aisle. On the subway, follow platform markings, keep priority seats available for people who need them, and step to the side if you must stop suddenly.

Wet floors, umbrellas, and crowded vehicles make these habits even more important during monsoon season. Our [Korea summer rain travel guide](/posts/korea-summer-rain-travel-guide/) explains what to carry and how to adjust a route when heavy rain affects walking and transit connections.

Use **저기요** to get attention; do not shout **야! (*ya!*)**, a casual call that can sound rude to a stranger. End help with **감사합니다 (*gamsahamnida*)**, thank you. If you bump someone or block the way, **죄송합니다 (*joesonghamnida*)** is the safer full apology.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the most useful Korean phrase on public transportation?**

Start with **저기요, [place]에 가요?** (*Jeogiyo, [place]-e gayo?*), meaning “Excuse me, does this go to [place]?” It works for both buses and trains when you point to the vehicle, platform, or map.

**Q: Is it rude to speak English to subway or bus staff in Korea?**

No. English ability varies, and asking politely is not rude. Begin with *jeogiyo*, use a short sentence, and show the Korean destination. Avoid assuming that every driver or passenger can follow a long English explanation.

**Q: How do I ask where to transfer in Korean?**

Say **어디에서 갈아타요?** (*Eodieseo garatayo?*). To name a line, say **2호선으로 어디에서 갈아타요?** (*I-hoseoneuro eodieseo garatayo?*), “Where do I transfer to Line 2?”

**Q: How do I ask a bus driver to tell me when to get off?**

Say **도착하면 알려 주세요** (*Dochakamyeon allyeo juseyo*), “Please let me know when we arrive.” Show the destination on your phone so the driver knows exactly which stop you mean, and still watch the route yourself.

**Q: What should I say when my transportation card does not work?**

Say **이 카드가 안 돼요** (*I kadeuga an dwaeyo*), “This card is not working.” At a service counter, hand over the card and add **도와주세요** (*dowajuseyo*), “Please help me.”

**Q: Can I rely on romanization instead of learning Hangul?**

Romanization is useful for a short trip, but it cannot represent every Korean sound cleanly. Save both Hangul and romanization, show the Hangul to the listener, and learn to recognize key words such as 역 (station), 출구 (exit), and 환승 (transfer).

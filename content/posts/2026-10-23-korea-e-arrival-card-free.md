**Korea's e-Arrival Card is free on the [official government website](https://www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr/).** If a page asks you to pay to submit that form, check the address before entering payment or passport details. First establish whether you need an arrival card: being **exempt from K-ETA** and **holding a valid K-ETA** lead to different answers.

The Ministry of Justice's [warning about imitation websites](https://immigration.go.kr/bbs/immigration_eng/229/593239/artclView.do), issued March 21, 2025, confirms that its official e-Arrival Card service never charges a fee. This guide checks the government instructions as of **October 23, 2026**.

## Do you need an arrival card if you have K-ETA?

Use this table for common situations, then check the [official declaration guide](https://e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/guide/eacTargetGuide.do) for your documents and entry category.

| Your situation on entry | Arrival-card decision |
|---|---|
| **Entering with a valid K-ETA** | Generally excluded from the arrival-card requirement. |
| **K-ETA exempt, without a valid K-ETA** | Exemption from K-ETA does **not** itself exempt you from the arrival card. |
| **Entering on an ordinary Korean visa** | Visa holders are generally subject to declaration. Having a K-ETA as well does not remove the requirement when entering with the visa. |
| **Valid Korean residence card or Korean citizenship** | These are excluded categories. |

Group visas and other special categories have separate exceptions; this table is not an exhaustive eligibility list. The government site's **Guide → Navigator** helps check the documents you hold.

The arrival card and K-ETA are [separate systems](https://e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/guide/eacGuide.do?locale=E). An arrival declaration does not replace a required visa or travel authorization. Before planning around an exemption, check the current requirements for your passport, purpose and travel date through the official sites linked in that guide or a Korean consulate.

## When should you submit? Count the Korean arrival date

The [official FAQ](https://e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/board/boardList.do?eacBbsKndCd=2) explains the submission window with a January 31 arrival: **January 29, 30 or 31**, before immigration. It uses **Korea Standard Time**. This is a window that includes arrival day, rather than an instruction to submit at least three days in advance.

Here is the same counting method applied to a hypothetical month-end trip:

| Arrival in Korea | Submission dates in Korea time |
|---|---|
| **October 31, 2026** | October 29, 30 or 31 |
| **November 1, 2026** | October 30, 31 or November 1 |
| **November 2, 2026** | October 31, November 1 or 2 |

Take the arrival date from your flight itinerary. Your departure date at home may be different. For a November 1 arrival, setting a reminder for October 30 **in Korea time** avoids treating October 29 as the first submission day. Confirm the dates offered by the official form.

The [government guide](https://e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/guide/eacGuide.do?locale=E) also states that a submitted card expires after **72 hours**, or after you complete Korean immigration. If a delay changes your arrival, check the submission's validity and update the details; an expired card requires a fresh submission. A later re-entry needs a new arrival card if you are still subject to declaration.

## Complete the official form without a payment step

Open **[e-arrivalcard.go.kr](https://www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr/)** directly and select English if needed. The [official procedure](https://e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/guide/eacReportProcessGuide.do) takes you through these stages:

1. **Read the terms and provide your email.** Use an address you can access during travel.
2. **Enter passport details.** Review every field after a passport scan; manual entry is available when the scan does not work.
3. **Enter the itinerary and stay information.** Have your flight details and accommodation address ready, and follow the fields shown for your trip.
4. **Review the information before submitting.** Check the passport number against the passport itself, and the arrival date against the Korea arrival on your ticket.
5. **Save the submission details.** The procedure provides a PDF and email record with an issue number.

For a family, the individual form supports **up to nine people**. The official procedure says someone **14 or younger cannot be the representative declarant**; that is a restriction on who completes the submission, not a statement that a child can never be included. Check each traveler's declaration category separately.

## What if the flight or hotel changes?

Use **Check/Edit** on the official website before immigration. The [guide](https://e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/guide/eacGuide.do?locale=E) allows changes to stay information, purpose and departure date; arrival-date corrections are limited to three days from the original submission. Check whether the existing card remains valid before relying on an edit after a substantial delay.

You do not have to present a printed e-Arrival Card at inspection, although saving the issue number can help. If you have not submitted before landing, the [FAQ](https://e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/board/boardList.do?eacBbsKndCd=2) says you can complete an electronic or paper arrival card before immigration. Completing the electronic form ahead of travel removes that task from your arrival routine; it does not guarantee entry or a shorter queue.

## A short departure checklist

- Check your **entry category**, including any visa or K-ETA requirement, separately from the arrival declaration.
- Open the **official government address** and check that the form is free.
- Use the **Korean arrival date** to choose your submission day.
- Compare the submitted **passport, flight and stay details** against your actual documents.
- Save the **issue number** and recheck the details if your plans change.

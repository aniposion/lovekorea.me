**Knowing that ㅈㅅ means “sorry” does not make it a good apology.** In an important message, write the full sentence: what happened, that you are sorry, and any practical next step. Shorthand is easier to recognize than to use appropriately.

The examples here are original teaching examples, not real private chats or a survey of 2026 usage. Online tone varies between people and communities.

## Five forms worth recognizing

| Form | Common reading in a casual chat | What to avoid assuming |
|---|---|---|
| ㅋㅋ | Laughter, often similar to “haha” | That a particular number of letters always means friendliness or sarcasm |
| ㅠㅠ / ㅜㅜ | A visual crying or emotional expression | That the sender is necessarily sad rather than moved, frustrated or playful |
| ㅇㅋ | “OK,” associated with 오케이 | That it has the politeness of 네, 알겠습니다 |
| ㄱㄱ | “Go/go ahead/let's start,” associated with 고고 | That it is a suitable instruction to an unfamiliar person |
| ㅈㅅ | A shortened apology associated with 죄송 | That a clipped apology communicates the care a serious situation needs |

These forms do not all follow the same construction. ㅠㅠ is a visual expression, while some others use selected consonants. Do not try to decode every unfamiliar string by mechanically taking the first letter of a guessed word.

## The full form matters

The National Institute of Korean Language's [dictionary entry for 죄송하다](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=91763&nation=eng) gives the underlying meaning and forms including 죄송합니다. The dictionary supports the full word; it is not a rulebook assigning one fixed tone to every slang abbreviation.

For the speech-level decision, use these paired examples:

| What you want to do | With a close friend, where casual speech is established | A polite, explicit alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Agree to a plan | 좋아, 그럼 7시에 보자. | 네, 좋아요. 그럼 7시에 뵐게요. |
| Confirm information | 응, 확인했어. | 네, 확인했습니다. |
| Ask to start | 이제 시작할까? | 이제 시작해도 될까요? |
| Apologize for a delay | 늦어서 미안해. | 늦어서 죄송합니다. |

The polite versions do more than replace letters: they state the intended action. With someone you have just met, choosing a full sentence avoids guessing whether shorthand is welcome. See the [honorifics guide](/posts/korean-honorifics-banmal-guide/) for speech levels.

## Same letters, different situation

**Example A: planning a casual game**

> A: 준비됐어? — Ready?
>
> B: 응, ㄱㄱ! — Yes, let's go!

Here the intended action is already shared. A short response is understandable.

**Example B: arriving late for an appointment**

> 늦어서 죄송합니다. 10분 정도 늦을 것 같습니다.
>
> I'm sorry I'm late. I expect to be about ten minutes late.

This gives the other person useful information. Adding ㅋㅋ would not repair the inconvenience and could make the message seem unserious. The ten minutes is part of this invented example; replace it with your actual estimate.

**Example C: reacting to a moving performance**

> 마지막 장면 보고 울었어 ㅠㅠ.
>
> I cried watching the final scene.

The surrounding sentence explains the emotion. Without that context, do not assume ㅠㅠ describes a crisis or a literal physical reaction.

## Practice: choose clarity over guessing

1. A hotel asks you to confirm an arrival time. Would you send ㅇㅋ or 네, 오후 6시쯤 도착할 예정입니다?
2. A close friend sends a funny pet video. Could ㅋㅋ make sense?
3. You missed a paid class. Is ㅈㅅ enough to explain what happened or ask about the next step?

**Answer key:** (1) The full sentence confirms an approximate 6 p.m. arrival. (2) Yes, if that matches your relationship and response; laughter has no mandatory letter count. (3) Use a full apology and a clear question. Shorthand leaves the practical issue unresolved.

You do not need slang to sound friendly. **고마워요!** (“Thank you!”) or **좋아요!** (“Sounds good!”) can express warmth without guessing at an unfamiliar chat style.

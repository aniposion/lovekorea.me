**Already decided that the KORAIL Pass fits your trip?** The purchase and the **seat reservation are separate steps**. KORAIL instructs travelers to buy the pass, choose the actual departure days, then reserve eligible trains through **My Ticket**. A pass alone does not reserve a specific KTX seat. The sequence below follows [KORAIL's official pass guide](https://www.korail.com/global/eng/passengerGuide/ticketTypes/korailpass), checked September 23, 2026. Screen labels and policies may change, so confirm them on KORAIL before paying.

If you have not compared prices yet, start with our [official KORAIL Pass fare table and break-even guide](/posts/korea-ktx-korail-pass-prices-2026/). This page focuses on **how to book and board**, avoiding a second, inconsistent price list.

## Before purchase: four checks

1. **Eligibility:** KORAIL offers this pass to foreign visitors; people with Korean nationality cannot use it under its published rules. Enter the name and passport details of the actual traveler, because the pass is not transferable.
2. **Train coverage:** Check that your planned journeys use eligible KORAIL trains. [KORAIL](https://www.korail.com/global/eng/passengerGuide/ticketTypes/korailpass) excludes **SRT, metropolitan subway and temporary tourist trains**.
3. **Travel days:** The Select Pass gives you **2, 3, 4 or 5 selected days within 10 days** from the start date. Count the dates on which you will board eligible trains, including your return leg.
4. **Seats and alternatives:** Look at individual ticket prices and available trains first. If you require a seat at a fixed time, check availability before treating the pass as a solution to a sold-out departure.

KORAIL's guide says a **valid passport and credit card** are needed to purchase the pass. Use the [official KORAIL site](https://www.korail.com/global) or a seller specifically identified by KORAIL, and review the total and refund conditions in that channel before final payment. A third-party platform may use different fulfillment steps.

## Purchase the pass

On KORAIL's [Pass page](https://www.korail.com/global/eng/passengerGuide/ticketTypes/korailpass), choose **Book KORAIL Pass**. Select the passenger type, pass duration and starting date that match your itinerary. Enter each traveler's passport information exactly as shown on the document. Review the currency, names and selected dates before paying.

Keep the pass number or the email address used at purchase. You will need it to find the pass in **My Ticket**. If you purchased through an authorized seller, follow that seller's instructions for registering or retrieving the pass; do not assume the seller's voucher is itself a reserved train seat.

KORAIL says the pass type, passenger type and personal information **cannot be changed after purchase**; its [FAQ](https://www.korail.com/global/eng/korailpass/faq) advises refunding and buying a new pass if those are wrong. Its published rules also say refunds are unavailable after the pass's starting date. Read the current conditions for your exact purchase before completing payment.

## Register travel days and reserve seats

The [KORAIL guide](https://www.korail.com/global/eng/passengerGuide/ticketTypes/korailpass) gives this order:

1. Open **My Ticket** and find your pass using the pass number or purchase email.
2. Open the pass **Details** and add each actual **departure date**. The dates must fit the Select Pass validity window.
3. Return to the pass list and choose **Seat Reservation**.
4. Search for your departure and arrival stations, date and train. Confirm that the selected train is eligible for the pass.
5. Choose an available standard-class seat, finish the reservation and retain the seat-reservation ticket alongside the pass.

KORAIL says reservations open **one month before departure** and allows **up to two reserved seats per person per day**. If you need to change a reserved train, cancel the old reservation under KORAIL's current rules before searching for another. First-class seats require an additional payment according to the official guide.

**A pass is not a promise of a seat.** KORAIL says that when reservations are sold out, a pass holder may use a standing place with a valid pass and passport under its rules. That may be unsuitable for a long ride, a traveler who must sit or a tightly timed connection. Check your particular train before relying on this option.

## At the station and on board

Carry the **same passport** used for the named pass. Have the valid pass and, if you reserved a seat, its reservation ticket ready to show. KORAIL says a ticket displayed through its mobile website is valid but **a screenshot of a train ticket is not**. Make sure you can load the original pass and reservation if your phone has limited connectivity or power; ask KORAIL about an acceptable alternative before travel if you cannot.

Check the departure **station**, not only the city. Seoul-area journeys may leave from Seoul, Yongsan, Cheongnyangni or another station; SRT normally uses different stations and is excluded from the pass. Leave time to reach the platform and verify the train number.

## Common booking mistakes

- Buying a pass before calculating whether separate tickets cost less. Use the [price comparison guide](/posts/korea-ktx-korail-pass-prices-2026/).
- Choosing two travel days when the third train journey falls on a different date.
- Treating Pass+ as free subway travel. The [tourism organization's Pass+ explainer](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=177&vcontsId=1563646) says its local-transport card requires a separate charge and top-up.
- Assuming a pass reserves a seat automatically, or assuming a sold-out reserved train will meet your accessibility or comfort needs.
- Presenting a screenshot in place of a valid pass or ticket.

If the official booking screen does not match this guide, follow KORAIL's live instructions or contact its customer service before paying. The [Korea Tourism Organization's train guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/infoBscView.do?vcontsId=140656) also explains the distinction between KORAIL trains, SRT and local transit.

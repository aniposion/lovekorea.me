**A combined KTX ticket is not a promise of one seat all the way.** It can join a seated segment to a standing segment, or two seated segments with a seat change. Read both parts and find the changeover station before paying, especially if someone in your party needs to sit throughout the journey.

KORAIL's [August 2022 explanation of combined tickets, 병합승차권](https://info.korail.com/info/selectBbsNttView.do?bbsNo=199&key=911&nttNo=16440), describes both **좌석+입석** (seat + standing) and **좌석+좌석** (seat + seat). That explains the product; it does not establish that a particular train or English booking screen offers it today. Check the actual ticket details or ask a station ticket office.

## Read the label before the price

| Ticket wording | What to check |
|---|---|
| Reserved seat / 좌석 | Train, carriage, seat number and the exact stations covered by that seat |
| Standing / 입석 | No assigned seat for the stated segment; do not plan on finding an empty one |
| Standing + seat / 입석+좌석 | Which segment is seated, which is standing, and where the change happens |
| Seat + seat / 좌석+좌석 | The seat assignment for each segment and where you must change seats |
| Unreserved / 자유석 | A designated car without an assigned seat; sitting is not assured |

KORAIL's [current Korean FAQ](https://www.korail.com/ticket/reserve/guide/faq), checked **October 22, 2026**, describes unreserved cars on designated weekday commuter services, excluding weekends and public holidays. It warns that busy services can require standing. This is a separate product, not a general rule that any sold-out weekend train has an unreserved car.

For a combined ticket, write down **date → train number → first segment and assignment → changeover station → second segment and assignment**. Check each traveler's ticket separately. Do not assume one person's seat assignment also covers a companion.

## Work out the standing time

**Invented example—not a timetable, fare quote or available train:** a trip runs from 14:00 to 16:40, with the changeover stop at 15:00.

| Assignment on the example ticket | Seated time | Standing time |
|---|---:|---:|
| Standing until 15:00, then seated | 100 minutes | 60 minutes |
| Seated until 15:00, then standing | 60 minutes | 100 minutes |
| Seated throughout, changing seats at 15:00 | 160 minutes | 0 minutes |

All three journeys take 160 minutes. The first two contain a seat assignment but differ by **40 minutes** of standing. The third has no planned standing segment but requires a seat change. Use the scheduled times at the actual segment boundary on your own ticket; one visible seat number alone does not answer the question.

Decide what your party can manage before comparing the total price. If a full-journey seat is essential, a later reserved departure may be a better fit than an earlier mixed ticket. Include the later arrival in your hotel, tour or flight plan. For luggage, check our [KTX two-piece packing guide](/posts/ktx-luggage-two-bag-rule/) before assuming bags can occupy a spare seat or aisle.

## What to do when your KTX search says sold out

1. **Check that you are searching an open sales date.** A future date outside the booking window is a different problem. Our [KTX release-calendar guide](/posts/ktx-booking-two-months-ahead/) explains that check.
2. **Look for another departure that fits the day.** Compare the arrival station and onward transfer as well as the train time. Recheck the return journey too.
3. **Ask about the specific sold-out train.** KORAIL's [FAQ](https://www.korail.com/ticket/reserve/guide/faq) directs passengers to station ticket offices for standing tickets, or to reservation waiting lists for cancelled seats. Ask what can actually be issued for your date; this is not a guarantee that a standing ticket remains available.
4. **If a combined ticket is offered, inspect every segment.** Ask which stop changes your seating arrangement. Check the train number too: changing seats is not itself a transfer to another train. Do not follow an old app screenshot if the current screen differs.
5. **Choose a workable backup.** Compare another train or, for that route, our [Seoul–Busan train and express-bus guide](/posts/seoul-busan-ktx-vs-express-bus/). Include the bus terminal's location and the trip to your accommodation.

At the counter, show your date and destination with **전 구간 앉아서 갈 수 있는 표가 있나요?**—“Is there a ticket with a seat for the whole journey?” For a mixed ticket, **어느 구간이 입석인가요?** asks “Which segment is standing?” These are practical language prompts, not promises of availability.

## A waitlist is not an issued ticket

The [current FAQ](https://www.korail.com/ticket/reserve/guide/faq) says online waiting-list applications are available until two days before departure. If a seat is allocated, its current payment deadline is **18:00 on the allocation day**; an unpaid allocation is cancelled. Check the deadline shown in your own allocation notice immediately. A separate [KORAIL mobile-help page](https://smart.letskorail.com/ebizmw/mwQna.do) still says midnight. Use the current booking FAQ and your allocation notice when deciding when to pay.

The Korean FAQ does not establish that every visitor can join a waiting list through every language, account or payment channel. If the option is unavailable to you, use an actual available ticket or ask the ticket office. KORAIL also requires a valid ticket to travel; a search result or waiting-list request is not permission to board.

**Already holding a KORAIL Pass?** Follow the separate [pass travel-day and seat-reservation checklist](/posts/korail-pass-prices-how-to-book/). Do not buy a pass merely because a normal seat search is full.

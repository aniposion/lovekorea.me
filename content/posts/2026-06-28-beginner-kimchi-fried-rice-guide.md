**Wet kimchi and extra sauce can overwhelm a small pan of rice.** Start with drained chopped kimchi, a measured portion of cooked rice and very little additional soy sauce. You can add seasoning after the rice is hot; you cannot easily remove it.

The quantities below are an editorial starting recipe, not a claim of test-kitchen validation. Kimchi varies in salt, heat and acidity, so the adjustment steps are part of the recipe.

## One-serving starting quantities

| Ingredient | Starting amount | Why it is limited |
|---|---:|---|
| Cooked rice | 200 g | Defines the portion instead of an unspecified “bowl” |
| Cabbage kimchi, chopped and lightly drained | 80 g | Gives a repeatable rice-to-kimchi starting point |
| Neutral cooking oil | 2 teaspoons | Add a little more only if the pan needs it |
| Chopped scallion | 1 tablespoon, optional | An optional aromatic |
| Soy sauce | ½ teaspoon, then adjust | Kimchi may already provide enough salt |
| Kimchi liquid | Reserve up to 1 teaspoon initially | Add only if the finished mixture needs moisture and flavor |
| Toasted sesame oil | ½ teaspoon, optional | Add at the end for aroma |
| Egg | 1, optional | Cook separately until white and yolk are firm |

Use kimchi and other ingredients that fit your dietary needs. Kimchi may contain seafood ingredients; soy sauce and sesame oil introduce additional allergen considerations. The product label, not the dish name, determines suitability.

## Handle the rice safely first

Do not leave cooked rice on the counter overnight to dry it. USDA guidance is to refrigerate perishables within **two hours**, or **one hour above 90°F/32°C**, using shallow containers for rapid cooling. Keep refrigerated leftovers at 40°F/4°C or below, use them within three to four days, and reheat to **165°F/74°C**. Discard leftovers held outside those limits; reheating is not a way to rescue mishandled food. [USDA leftovers guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety), checked October 3, 2026.

Refrigerated rice can be loosened with a spoon before cooking. Freshly cooked rice also works: cook the dish promptly and control the added liquid instead of waiting for it to become cold on the counter. For packaged rice, follow its preparation instructions.

## Method

1. **Prepare before heating.** Measure the rice, chop and drain the kimchi, and keep additional sauce separate.
2. **Warm the pan over medium heat.** Add the oil and optional scallion; stir briefly without browning it heavily.
3. **Cook the kimchi first.** Stir for roughly two to three minutes, until the pan no longer holds a pool of loose liquid. Pan size and kimchi moisture affect this step.
4. **Add the rice.** Break up clumps gently with the spatula, mixing until the kimchi is distributed. Avoid grinding the rice into the pan.
5. **Season cautiously.** Add the initial soy sauce and continue stirring. Check that reheated rice reaches 165°F/74°C throughout before tasting and serving.
6. **Adjust once it is hot.** Add a few drops more soy sauce only if needed. If too dry, add the reserved kimchi liquid a little at a time. Stir and heat through after additions.
7. **Finish.** Take off the heat, add optional sesame oil, and serve with the separately cooked egg if using.

Use a food thermometer rather than assuming a fixed number of minutes guarantees reheating. For egg safety, the [USDA food-safety basics](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/steps-keep-food-safe) give guidance for cooking eggs and egg dishes.

## Diagnose one problem at a time

| Result | Next adjustment | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Wet or heavy | Let excess liquid evaporate before adding rice; use a wider pan or a smaller batch | Pouring in more sauce for color |
| Too salty | Serve with additional plain rice or make the next batch with less soy sauce | Adding salty processed meat as a “fix” |
| Too sour for your preference | Try a small pinch of sugar in a spoonful first, then decide whether to adjust the pan | Sweetening the whole batch before tasting |
| Too spicy | Increase plain rice and reduce spicy kimchi in the next batch | Assuming cheese removes the heat for everyone |
| Clumpy | Loosen the rice before cooking and separate gently | Mashing harder |

Do not add raw meat to the finished rice as an improvised topping. Cook any meat separately to its required safe temperature. For a first attempt, the rice-and-kimchi base is easier to troubleshoot without several salty add-ins.

Record the rice weight, kimchi brand, amount drained and final soy sauce quantity. That gives you a recipe adjusted to your actual ingredients rather than a promise that one sauce ratio fits every kimchi.

**A refundable KTX booking may still leave you paying a reservation fee.** Rail Ninja's [terms, section 6.1](https://rail.ninja/terms-and-conditions), say its reservation fees remain non-refundable when you cancel. Before choosing it or KORAIL, compare the complete price and the amount you would actually recover. Already bought a ticket? A cheaper replacement can still make the switch more expensive.

This guide covers **voluntary cancellation of an ordinary individual train booking**. Disrupted services and rail passes need their own conditions. Official sources checked **October 24, 2026, US Central time**; use the terms attached to your purchase for an existing order.

## Start with the actual seller

For a direct purchase, start at [KORAIL's official ticketing guide](https://www.korail.com/global/eng/passengerGuide/ticketTypes/tickets). It lists the KORAIL website, KORAIL+ app, railway ticket machines and station counters. The [Consumers Association of Singapore's alert](https://www.case.org.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Alert-on-Unauthorised-Train-Ticket-Websites-in-Korea.pdf) also identifies **korail.com** as the official site.

Look at the domain and seller on the final order page. A heading containing “KTX” or “Korea trains” is not enough to identify who will take your payment and handle a cancellation.

### What changes with the booking channel?

| Check | Direct KORAIL web ticket | Rail Ninja booking |
|---|---|---|
| Price to compare | The quote for your exact train and passengers | The complete seller quote; its section 5.3 says fees are included and can vary |
| Voluntary refund | Timing and travel-day category affect KORAIL's deduction | Your selected fare sets eligibility and deadline; section 6.1 retains reservation fees |
| Where to start | MY TICKET before departure | Your Rail Ninja account and the order's cancellation conditions |

Sources: [KORAIL ticketing guide](https://www.korail.com/global/eng/passengerGuide/ticketTypes/tickets) and [Rail Ninja terms, sections 5.3 and 6.1–6.2](https://rail.ninja/terms-and-conditions). Direct booking does not make every cancellation free. The seller's conditions for your specific order matter; do not apply this example to every booking platform.

## Compare the same journey before paying

Write down both offers with **the same departure date, train number, boarding station, destination, class and passenger ages**. Confirm that each offers the seating arrangement you need.

Then record:

- **Total payment and currency**, including every selected extra.
- **Net refund if cancelled at a specific time**, after all retained amounts.
- **Cancellation deadline and time zone**.
- **Ticket status:** an issued ticket, or an order still waiting for confirmation.

If one site's date is available and another's is not, inspect the [KTX booking window and special release notices](/posts/ktx-booking-two-months-ahead/) before paying. Ask what happens if the requested train cannot be issued. A quote alone does not tell you the final ticket status.

## Already booked? Calculate the cost of switching

Use this calculation for a **usable existing ticket that you would otherwise keep**:

**Total after switching = original payment − confirmed net refund + replacement price.**

These are **hypothetical amounts in US dollars**, not current KTX prices or Rail Ninja refund quotations. Assume you already paid **$90** and find a suitable replacement costing **$60**, with no additional bank or currency costs.

| Net refund offered | Total after switching | Compared with keeping the $90 ticket |
|---:|---:|---|
| $50 | $90 − $50 + $60 = **$100** | Costs **$10 more** |
| $70 | $90 − $70 + $60 = **$80** | Saves **$10** |

The $30 difference between the two ticket prices is not your saving. In this example, switching saves money only when the **net refund exceeds $60**, the replacement cost. Include any additional payment or currency costs in your real calculation. If the quoted refund already deducts the fees, do not subtract them a second time.

Before cancelling, confirm the replacement's current availability and the old order's exact refund. Availability can change while you switch. Keep the cancellation confirmation and check that the refund eventually reaches your payment account.

A useful message to the seller is:

> Before I cancel, please confirm the exact amount and currency returned to my original payment method, every retained fee, and the cancellation deadline with its time zone. Is this an issued ticket or an order awaiting confirmation?

## If departure is close

For direct web tickets, KORAIL's [guide](https://www.korail.com/global/eng/passengerGuide/ticketTypes/tickets) sends customers to the station counter after departure and rules out a normal refund after the ticketed arrival time. Our [missed-KTX guide](/posts/missed-ktx-train-refund/) explains that timing and the narrower app exception.

For a seller-issued booking, use that seller's cancellation route promptly; do not assume KORAIL's direct-purchase process handles the whole seller payment. If you are at the station, show staff the issued ticket and ask what they can resolve.

Finish your booking check by confirming [Seoul versus Yongsan station](/posts/ktx-seoul-station-or-yongsan/). A clear refund policy is useful, but arriving at the correct departure station is better than needing it.

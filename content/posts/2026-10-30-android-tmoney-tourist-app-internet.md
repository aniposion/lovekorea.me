**The tourist-focused KOREA TOUR CARD Tmoney app for Android requires a data or Wi-Fi connection as well as NFC.** Its [official Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tmoney.mkoreatourcard&hl=en_US) states that requirement. If your arrival plan relies on airport Wi-Fi and no mobile connection afterward, prepare another way to pay before heading to the subway.

Official sources checked **October 30, 2026**. This guide compares published requirements; it does not claim a payment test on your phone model.

## Choose your first-ride plan

| Your situation | Practical choice | What to settle before travel |
|---|---|---|
| You want to tap an Android phone and will have mobile data | Investigate the official tourist app below | Installation, account setup, funding and the payment screen must all work on your device |
| You already use Samsung Wallet | Check its actual Korean transit-card options separately | A working shopping card in Wallet is not confirmation of a usable T-money transit card |
| You want the fewest phone dependencies | Buy a physical T-money card | Have Korean won available for loading and confirm the balance |
| The flight is soon and app setup is still unresolved | Plan around the physical card | Troubleshoot the app later, away from the gate queue |

The recommendation is to choose by the setup you can complete, rather than the wallet logo on your home screen. A successful app download alone is not a finished transit-payment plan.

## Find the tourist app, not just an app with “Tmoney” in its name

Look for **KOREA TOUR CARD Tmoney** by **(주)티머니** using the direct Play link above.

[Tmoney's mobile-service page](https://eng.tmoney.co.kr/en/aeb/solution/contents/contents5.dev) describes this particular app as a service designed for foreign travelers, with an account, phone-based transit payment, and in-app loading and refunds. It uses a technology called HCE to address restrictions associated with foreign USIM cards. That description is useful evidence that a tourist option exists; it is not a promise that every overseas handset will install or run it successfully.

Tmoney's service page and the Play listing give these prerequisites:

| Published requirement | Check on your own phone |
|---|---|
| Android 5.0.1 or later | Check the current store's compatibility result, not only the old minimum version |
| NFC read/write mode enabled | Confirm your model has NFC and that it is switched on |
| Data or Wi-Fi | Plan connectivity for the journey, beyond the airport |

Play showed an **April 15, 2025** update date at our check. If installation is unavailable, ask the listed developer for help or use a physical card.

## Check the payment total before funding

The app's published funding list includes **Mastercard, Visa, American Express, JCB and UnionPay** credit or debit cards. This is a network list, not a guarantee for every issuing bank. The same listing describes in-app refunds but does not give enough detail to quote a universal current fee or refund timetable.

Before approving a load, compare:

- **Transit credit:** how much spendable balance you are adding.
- **Total charge:** the amount the app asks you to pay, including any displayed service charge.
- **Your issuer's terms:** any separate overseas transaction or currency-conversion charge.

Start with an amount suited to your immediate plans rather than loading the whole holiday budget before the first ride. After payment, check the balance and transaction status. If a charge appears without the expected credit, retain the confirmation and resolve that mismatch before repeating the payment.

This is a payment-checking method, not a quoted fee schedule. A low advertised top-up amount is only useful if the amount charged and the credit received are clear.

## Samsung Wallet is a separate route

Do not apply the tourist app's requirements to every Samsung transit product. Samsung's [Korean transit-card guide](https://www.samsungsvc.co.kr/solution/1003676), dated **August 26, 2026**, describes an **in-app storage** option that can work with an eSIM or ordinary USIM; it does not always require an NFC USIM. In that mode, T-money is prepaid. Samsung describes a connection requirement with a limited offline allowance of up to four uses for that mode.

That allowance belongs to the Samsung implementation described on that page. It is not evidence of the same allowance in KOREA TOUR CARD Tmoney.

For a US-bought Galaxy, first check what your actual Wallet offers. Samsung's [US FAQ](https://www.samsung.com/us/apps/samsung-wallet/faq/) says availability varies with country or region, device, carrier and firmware. The Korean instructions therefore do not establish eligibility for every US device and payment card.

**Only rely on this route after you can issue and fund the appropriate transit card.** If the option is absent, keep a workable alternative rather than assuming it will appear when you land.

## Keep the physical-card fallback simple

Tmoney's [official visitor guide](https://blog.tmoney.co.kr/en/tmoney-guide/tmoney-convenience-store-guide/) says a standard physical card can be used without an app or registration. Cards are sold at convenience stores and other designated outlets, with loading available at convenience stores and subway stations. It recommends cash as the reliable fallback for physical-card reloads; an app's foreign-card support does not establish the same support at a counter.

Buy the card, ask to load the amount you need in Korean won, and confirm the resulting balance. Keep it separate from the phone when presenting either to a reader: Samsung warns that touching a plastic transit card and a phone together can cause two cards to be detected.

One naming trap deserves attention. The official [Tmoney tourist-card page](https://eng.tmoney.co.kr/aeb/biz/bridge/foreignTravel.dev) says sales of the **physical KOREA TOUR CARD** have ended and now presents TMONEY TRAVEL CARD products. Meanwhile, the mobile-service page and Google Play still list **KOREA TOUR CARD Tmoney**. The physical-card sales notice by itself does not establish that the Android app has closed. Follow the specific product's current guidance.

## Before you leave the airport

Use this quick readiness check:

1. **Choose one payment method for the first journey.** Know whether you will present the phone or a funded physical card.
2. **Open the relevant app away from the queue.** Check its current instructions and balance; keep the phone charged.
3. **Resolve connectivity now.** If you choose the tourist app, use our [Korea eSIM and SIM guide](/posts/korea-esim-sim-incheon-airport-price-guide/) to check your phone and plan before relying on it outside Wi-Fi coverage.
4. **Check the transport product too.** A transit card and an assigned-seat train ticket are different things. Our [AREX Express versus All-Stop guide](/posts/incheon-airport-arex-guide/) explains which airport train needs a separate ticket.

Traveling with an iPhone user? Their setup is covered in the [iPhone T-money funding guide](/posts/iphone-tmoney-foreign-card-top-up/). For a group, also check [why each subway traveler needs a separate fare medium](/posts/one-tmoney-card-two-people/) before placing all the travel money on one device.
